## Exercise 6: A System with a Parameter

System:[cite: 15]
1) $x + y = 2$[cite: 15]
2) $ax + ay = 4 \implies a(x + y) = 4$[cite: 15]

### Analysis
Substitute Eq 1 into Eq 2: $a(2) = 4 \implies 2a = 4 \implies a = 2$.[cite: 15]

* **If $a = 2$:** The second equation becomes $2x + 2y = 4$, which is just a multiple of the first equation.[cite: 15] They are the same line. Result: **Infinitely many solutions.**[cite: 15]
* **If $a \neq 2$:** The equations contradict each other (e.g., if $a=1$, then $x+y=2$ and $x+y=4$).[cite: 15] Result: **No solution.**[cite: 15]

### Conclusion
"Exactly one solution" cannot occur here because the coefficients of $x$ and $y$ in the second equation are always proportional to those in the first equation.[cite: 15] The lines are either parallel or identical, never intersecting at just one point.
