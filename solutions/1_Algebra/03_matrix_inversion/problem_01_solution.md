## Exercise 1: Definition of the Inverse

### Computations
* $AB = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2(1)+1(-1) & 2(-1)+1(2) \\ 1(1)+1(-1) & 1(-1)+1(2) \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = I$[cite: 13]
* $BA = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1(2)+(-1)(1) & 1(1)+(-1)(1) \\ -1(2)+2(1) & -1(1)+2(1) \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = I$[cite: 13]

### Conclusion
By definition, a matrix $B$ is the inverse of $A$ if $AB = I$ and $BA = I$.[cite: 13] Since both products equal the identity matrix, $B = A^{-1}$.[cite: 13]
