## Exercise 10: Detecting Dependence

Matrix: $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 2 & 4 & 6 \\ 0 & 1 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$[cite: 11]

### Explanation
By looking at the matrix, we can see that Row 2 is exactly 2 times Row 1 ($R_2 = 2R_1$).[cite: 11]

### Connection to noninvertibility
This specific dependence means the rows are linearly dependent.[cite: 11] When a matrix has linearly dependent rows, its determinant is automatically 0, meaning the matrix is not invertible.[cite: 11]
