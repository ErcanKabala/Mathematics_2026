## Exercise 8: Determinant of a Product

Given $\det A = 3$ and $\det B = -5$:[cite: 11]

* $\det(AB) = \det A \cdot \det B = 3 \cdot (-5) = -15$[cite: 11]
* $\det(BA) = \det B \cdot \det A = (-5) \cdot 3 = -15$[cite: 11]
* $\det(A^{-1}B) = \det(A^{-1}) \cdot \det B = (1/3) \cdot (-5) = -5/3$[cite: 11]

### Why $\det(AB) = \det(BA)$
Determinants are scalar numbers. Even though matrix multiplication is not commutative ($AB \neq BA$), the multiplication of their scalar determinants is ($3 \cdot -5 = -5 \cdot 3$).[cite: 11]
